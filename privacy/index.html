NaviMate — Privacy Policy

Last updated 26 September 2026

NaviMate is a walking-tour app that writes your tour as you walk, using Google's Gemini AI. This policy explains exactly what leaves your iPhone, who receives it, and why. It is short because the app does very little with your data.

The short version

  • There is no NaviMate account, and no NaviMate server that stores anything about you.
  • Your saved tours, journal entries and photographs stay on your iPhone.
  • There is no tracking: no advertising identifier, no analytics SDK, no third-party trackers, and nothing shared with data brokers.
  • Two companies receive data, and only to make the app work: Google (to write your tours) and Apple (to turn your speech into text).
  • Nothing is sold, and nothing is used for advertising.

What is collected, and who receives it

DataWhenWho receives itWhy
Precise location While a tour or an Explore session is running Google To find landmarks near you and to write narration about where you actually are. Your coordinates are included in the request sent to the Gemini API, and in map and place lookups.
Photographs Only when you take a picture with Landmark Lens Google The single photograph is uploaded so the model can identify what you are looking at. Nothing is uploaded unless you deliberately take a picture.
Microphone audio While you are speaking to your guide Apple Speech recognition uses Apple's Speech framework, which sends audio to Apple's servers to transcribe it. See the note below — this one surprises people.
Your words, as text When you ask a question, type a tour theme, or write a journal note Google So your guide can answer you, and so your tour matches the theme you asked for.

About the microphone, specifically

This is worth stating plainly, because the obvious assumption is wrong in one direction and right in the other.

  • Your voice audio does go to Apple. NaviMate uses SFSpeechRecognizer, which by default performs recognition on Apple's servers rather than on the device. This is covered by Apple's own privacy policy, not ours.
  • Your voice audio does not go to Google. Only the recognised text is sent onward to Gemini. Your guide never receives the recording.
  • The microphone is only live while a tour or Explore session is running, and you can mute it at any time from the on-screen control.

What stays on your iPhone

Saved tours, your walk journal, postcards, downloaded narration audio and any photographs you took are stored in the app's own container on the device. They are not uploaded anywhere. If you have iCloud Backup enabled, they may be included in your device backup, which is between you and Apple.

Children

NaviMate is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect data from children under 13.

AI-generated content

Tour narration is written by a large language model at the moment you walk. It is not written or reviewed by a human, and it can be wrong — confidently and plausibly wrong — about dates, names and buildings. Treat it as an enthusiastic guide rather than a reference work, and check anything that matters.

Content filters are applied to every request, and every story in the app has a Report this story button beneath it. Reports go to the support address below and are read by a human.

Your choices

  • Revoke access to location, microphone, camera or speech recognition at any time in iOS Settings. The features that depend on them will stop working, and the rest of the app will continue.
  • Delete individual tours from the History tab.
  • Delete everything by deleting the app. Because nothing is stored on a server, that is genuinely all of it.
  • Re-read this disclosure in the app at any time: Settings → Privacy & Support.

Third-party processing

Requests are sent to Google's Gemini API and are handled under Google's Gemini API terms and privacy policy. Speech recognition is handled under Apple's privacy policy. NaviMate has no other third-party SDKs of any kind.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that affects what is collected or who receives it, the in-app disclosure will be shown again before the change takes effect.
